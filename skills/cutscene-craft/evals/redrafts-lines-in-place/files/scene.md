# The Long Table: "Table Nine"

The Long Table is a cooking game. The player runs the night kitchen of a small restaurant. This
cutscene plays ten minutes before closing, on the last night of the season.

**The pictures are painted and the timings are cut to them. Only the words can change, and each
line has to fit its cut.**

## The scene

| # | Time | Picture | Lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 s | The pass. The last ticket of the night goes on the spike. Dario is already untying his apron. | DARIO: That's the last order. We're finally done for the night. |
| 2 | 3–7 s | The door. A woman in a grey coat sits down at table nine and lays a notebook beside her plate. | PIP: A woman just sat down at table nine! She has a notebook! |
| 3 | 7–11 s | Su, at the stove, doesn't turn round. | SU: I am very worried. That is the critic from the Courier. |
| 4 | 11–15 s | Dario ties the apron back on, badly. | DARIO: As you know, Chef, she closed your last restaurant with one review. |
| 5 | 15–19 s | Su takes the one clean pan down from its hook. | SU: We have to work together and do our best. |
| 6 | 19–23 s | Pip holds out the fish order. His hand shakes. | PIP: I'm scared, Chef. What if we fail? |
| 7 | 23–27 s | Su puts the pan in Pip's hand and closes his fingers round the handle. | SU: I believe in you, Pip. You can do this. |
| 8 | 27–30 s | Table nine. The woman uncaps her pen. | DARIO: Here we go. Let's show her what we can do. |

## Who they are

- **Su Min-ah,** sixties, the chef. She never raises her voice. She gives orders as times and
  amounts. She doesn't praise and doesn't explain, and the more it matters the fewer words she
  uses. She calls nobody by name in the kitchen.
- **Dario,** forties, her sous-chef. He cooked at her last restaurant. He talks too much, jokes
  when he is nervous, and brings up the past and then wishes he hadn't.
- **Pip,** sixteen, the dishwasher, three weeks in. He asks questions and apologises. He uses the
  kitchen's words a little wrong.

## What the scene has to do

Su decides, without saying so, that Pip will cook the critic's fish.
