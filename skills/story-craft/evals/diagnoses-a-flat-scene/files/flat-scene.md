# The Ferry Office

Marta woke at six, as she did every morning, and made coffee. The kitchen was cold. She looked out
at the harbour, where the gulls were already circling, and thought about the long day ahead. After
breakfast she walked down the hill to the ferry office, past the bakery and the shuttered
chandlery, and let herself in.

Jonas was already at his desk. "Morning," he said.

"Morning," said Marta. She hung up her coat. "We should talk about the route."

"Yes," said Jonas. "As you know, the company has run the island route for forty years, ever since
your father started it with one boat. Passenger numbers have fallen every year since the bridge
opened in the north."

"That's true," said Marta. "I feel sad about it. I have always been afraid of letting my father
down, and I think that is why I find it so hard to make decisions."

"I understand," said Jonas. "I feel the same. We both want what is best for the company."

They looked at the timetable together for a while. Jonas made more coffee.

"By the way," he said, "I found out last week that the harbour master is my cousin. Funny, isn't
it?"

"Funny," Marta agreed. They went back to the timetable.

By noon they had agreed that something would have to be done at some point, perhaps in the spring.
Marta felt better for having talked. She realised that as long as they faced things together,
everything would turn out all right, and that her father would have been proud of her after all.
She smiled, picked up her coat, and walked home through the quiet town.
